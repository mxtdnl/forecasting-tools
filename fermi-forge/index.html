Fermi ForgeBreaking big questions down — decomposition, error cancellation, sensitivity

Introduction

Enrico Fermi was famous for producing usable estimates of quantities he could not possibly know — the number of piano tuners in Chicago, the yield of the first atomic bomb — by decomposing an impossible question into a chain of merely difficult ones. This simulation asks you to do the same, four times, with increasing freedom.

You will: (A) estimate the number of gas boiler service engineers in Greater London, first by intuition and then by decomposition; (B) re-enact Fermi's Trinity paper-drop measurement; (C) decompose a geopolitical question in the style of the Good Judgment Project; and (D) explore why the Drake equation is useful despite producing no reliable answer.

Module A — Reveal

A caution on the "reference" value. The Gas Safe Register reported 150,729 registered gas engineers in the UK as at 31 March 2024 (At a Glance Report 2023/24, gassaferegister.co.uk, retrieved July 2026). Scaling by Greater London's share of the UK population (~13%) gives roughly 19,700 registered engineers, of whom perhaps half work chiefly on domestic boiler servicing — a reference of about 10,000. This reference is itself an estimate: registered engineers are not identical to active London domestic-service engineers, exactly as Fermi's "true" count of Chicago piano tuners was soft. Fermi estimates are judged on order of magnitude, and so are you.

Scoring: log error E = |log₁₀(estimate ÷ reference)|. E < 0.5 excellent; 0.5–1.0 within an order of magnitude (good); 1.0–1.5 fair; above 1.5 poor.

Module B — Trinity Paper Drop

When the blast wave arrives — some 40 seconds after the flash — he drops them and watches how far the wave carries them. From that single displacement he estimates the yield.

Module D — Drake Equation Uncertainty Tornado

Frank Drake's 1961 Green Bank equation estimates N, the number of communicating civilisations in the galaxy, as the product of seven factors. Its value is not the number it produces. Set a range (minimum and maximum) for each factor; the simulation samples each factor log-uniformly within your range, 2,000 times.

Tornado chart: reduction in output log-variance when each factor is frozen at its median, one at a time. The equation does not produce a reliable number; it makes explicit which factor drives the uncertainty — and therefore which factor is worth researching.

The payoff: pick the factor at the top of the tornado, narrow its range above, and run again. Watch whether the output spread collapses. Then narrow a factor at the bottom and note the difference.

Debrief

The pedagogical point

No individual estimate in this session needed to be accurate. The discipline lies in three places: the decomposition itself (Module A and C — replacing one impossible question with several merely difficult ones), the independence of errors (Module A's demonstrator — independent overestimates and underestimates partially cancel, whereas correlated bias compounds), and sensitivity analysis (Module D — knowing which factor to go and research). Module A is a London-set restaging of Fermi's original Chicago piano-tuner problem, which remains the canonical statement of the method: a household-owned asset, requiring periodic professional service, estimated through a chain of household factors.
