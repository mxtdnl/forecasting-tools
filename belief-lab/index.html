Belief LabActively open-minded thinking — beliefs as hypotheses, not treasures

Introduction

In the Good Judgment Project, the strongest psychological predictor of forecasting accuracy was not intelligence, nor subject expertise, but actively open-minded thinking — the disposition to treat beliefs as hypotheses to be tested, not treasures to be guarded. This laboratory measures your own updating behaviour, then shows that open-mindedness can be engineered structurally, not just willed individually.

Module A — Evidence Stream

Scenario (fictional). You are an analyst at a strategy desk. Your standing thesis: "Meridian Robotics will win the Helios defence contract." Over eight rounds you may draw evidence from one of two desks, then must restate your probability that the thesis is true.

The Supporting desk is free. The Red-team desk — which actively hunts for what would prove you wrong — costs 1 effort token; you have 5. Disconfirming information is costly to seek and easy to lose; Darwin kept a notebook specifically for observations that contradicted his theory, because he knew he would otherwise forget them.

Module B — The Soros Reversal

Vignette (fictional). You are long the Auria crown on the thesis that its central bank will defend the peg. Six signals arrive, one per round. Each round choose: Hold, Add (increase the position), or Reverse (flip it). Two desks trade beside you: the Anchored Trader, who never reverses and averages down when losing, and the Fallibilist, who is explicitly searching for the flaw in its own thesis and reverses whenever the cumulative evidence crosses a threshold.

Debrief

The pedagogical point

"Beliefs are hypotheses to be tested, not treasures to be guarded." In the Good Judgment Project data, actively open-minded thinking — not intelligence or expertise — was the strongest psychological predictor of accuracy. Module A measured your own updating: whether you paid for disconfirming evidence, and whether you moved as far when the evidence went against your thesis as when it flattered it. Module B showed the operational form of fallibilism: reversing without ego cost. Module C showed that where individuals fail, structure can succeed — doubts were suppressed by conformity pressure at the Bay of Pigs, and Kennedy's structural fixes for the Cuban Missile Crisis (stating his view last, assigned devil's advocates, independent sub-groups, mixed membership) are the toggles you just tested. Open-mindedness failures are fixed structurally at least as much as psychologically.
