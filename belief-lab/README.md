# Belief Lab

**Live:** https://mxtdnl.github.io/belief-lab/

An interactive classroom simulation on *actively open-minded thinking* (AOT), built as a single self-contained
HTML file with no external dependencies. Three modules: (A) Evidence Stream — eight rounds of choosing between
free confirmatory evidence and costly red-team evidence, with the player's updating behaviour silently logged
and scored (mean Brier, asymmetry index, search ratio); (B) The Soros Reversal — a six-round trading vignette
against an Anchored Trader bot that never reverses and a Fallibilist bot that searches for the flaw in its own
thesis; (C) Structuring the Room — the Bay of Pigs → ExCom natural experiment as four structural toggles and a
200-run Monte Carlo of decision quality. An optional six-item self-check (original items, written in the
spirit of the published AOT scale but not reproducing it) is echoed back against measured behaviour at the
debrief.

## Purpose

To teach three points: AOT — not intelligence or expertise — was the strongest psychological predictor of Good
Judgment Project accuracy; confirmation bias is measurable in one's own updating behaviour; and
open-mindedness can be engineered structurally, not just willed individually.

## Instructor guide

Allow 20–25 minutes including the self-check. Set a shared seed (top right) so the whole class faces the same
evidence and the same hidden outcome; results are then directly comparable when students paste their "Copy
results" summaries into Zoom chat. The reliable classroom moments are: the debrief verdict comparing each
student's *self-reported* open-mindedness with their *measured* asymmetry index and search ratio (the gap is
the lesson); the Fallibilist bot beating the Anchored Trader over the class's spread of seeds even though it
"changes its mind" constantly (calibration: Fallibilist mean P&L +7.7 vs Anchored −11.2 across 40 seeds); and
Module C's histogram showing that the default configuration — the Bay of Pigs room — is the worst one
available. Passive click-through play lands in the middle grade band by design.
